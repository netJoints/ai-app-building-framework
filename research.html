Pricing

A price is a research result. It is the amount a specific buyer will pay for a specific outcome, compared with what they do today. Pick the number after the conversations, and write the assumption next to it.

Answer four questions before anyone builds the payment flow.

  • Who pays. The person using the app, a manager buying for a group, or someone else.
  • What they pay for. A seat, a workspace, a result, or usage.
  • When they pay. Once, on a subscription, or by use.
  • What they do instead. The current workaround, and what it costs in money and time. That comparison is what the buyer is actually making.

On this stack, Stripe holds charges, subscriptions, and payment state. Firebase Functions creates the charge. Card details and provider keys stay with Stripe and in GitHub Actions secrets. They do not go in the repository or in the mobile app.

For product managers. Talk to people in the niche before the build. Ask what they pay now, what they tried, and what would make them switch. Record the answers. A price that has never been said out loud to a buyer is still a guess.

Viability

Viability asks whether the market you can actually win is large enough to justify building. Three nested markets keep the claim honest. Each one is smaller than the one before it.

Term Name What it counts
TAM Total Addressable Market Everyone who could buy this kind of product, with no niche filter.
SAM Serviceable Addressable Market The part of the TAM that matches this product’s specific niche: the buyers this product can actually serve.
SOM Serviceable Obtainable Market The part of the SAM the team can realistically reach and win. Serviceable, and obtainable.
TAM · total market → SAM · our niche → SOM · what we can win

Write four things beside every number: who is counted, the time window, the source, and the filter that removed everyone else. A TAM with no source is a slogan. A SOM with no time window is a wish.

For product managers. The build decision uses SOM, not TAM. A huge total market can still hide a niche the team cannot reach. The break-even count below is the number of paying customers that niche has to cover.

Break-even cost calculation

Break-even is the point where money from customers covers the cost of building and running the app. Pick one period, a month or the first year, and use that same period on every line.

Cost What to include How it usually behaves
Hosting and related services Firebase Hosting, Firebase Functions, Firestore, Neon, Firebase Auth, and the other services the app needs to stay up. A base plan is paid even with few users. Some usage grows as users grow.
Claude Code The Claude Code plan used to build and maintain the app, plus usage beyond that plan. The plan is a cost for the period. Extra usage grows with how much the team builds.
Domain and SMS verification The domain name, and SMS verification when a user confirms a phone number. The domain is usually fixed. SMS usually grows with each user who verifies.
Marketing What it costs to reach the buyers counted in the SOM. Write down what is included. A campaign can be fixed for the period. Some spend grows with each customer acquired.

Split each line into two piles. Costs you pay anyway for the period. Costs that grow with one more paying customer, such as a Stripe fee on the charge, an SMS verification, or hosting that scales per user.

Money kept from one customer
  = price from step 4 − costs that grow with that customer

Customers to break even
  = costs you pay anyway ÷ money kept from one customer

Compare that customer count with the SOM for the same period. If the SOM is smaller than the break-even count, the niche the team can win does not cover the cost. That is a reason to change the price, the scope, or the decision to build.

Write the quote or the source beside every amount. Leave a line blank until you have one. A blank is not zero.
